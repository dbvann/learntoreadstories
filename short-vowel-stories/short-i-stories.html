Short I Stories for Beginning Readers

Short I says /ĭ/ like igloo. These tiny stories use beginner-friendly words such as pig, sit, big, pin, and six.

What Is the Short I Sound?

The short I sound is the vowel sound you hear in words such as pig, sit, pin, and big. It appears in many simple words that beginning readers can learn to sound out.

When a child reads a word like sit, they can say each sound — s-i-t — and then blend the sounds together. Practicing short I words helps children get comfortable decoding before they meet the same words in a story.

Short I Words for Beginning Readers

Short I words often appear in simple word families. Practicing words with the same ending helps new readers notice patterns and blend sounds more easily.

-ig familypig, big, dig, wig
-it familysit, hit, fit, pit
-in familypin, tin, win, fin
-ip familylip, tip, dip, sip

How to Practice Short I at Home

Short reading practice works best when it stays simple. You do not need a long lesson or a large stack of flash cards. A few words and one short story can be enough.

👂Say the sound

Say the short I sound together, like the I in igloo.

🔤Read a few words

Try three to six short I words such as pig, sit, or pin.

📖Read one story

Choose one short I story and help your child sound out words when needed.

🌙Stop while it feels good

Reread the story if your child wants to. Then celebrate and be done for the day.
