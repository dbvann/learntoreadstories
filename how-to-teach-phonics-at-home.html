How to Teach Phonics at Home

Start with one letter sound, blend a few short words, read one tiny story, and stop while your child still feels successful.

Start with letter sounds

Phonics teaches children how written letters connect to spoken sounds. Begin with a few useful letter sounds rather than trying to teach the whole alphabet at once.

Blend short words

Once your child knows a few sounds, slide them together slowly. For example, /m/ /a/ /p/ becomes map. This turns sound knowledge into real reading.

Use decodable stories

Choose words and stories that use patterns your child has already learned. Short decodable stories help children practice instead of guessing from pictures.

Add a few sight words

Introduce only the common words needed for the story. Our guide to sight words explains how to handle tricky parts without overusing flashcards.

Keep practice short

  • Review one sound.
  • Read three to six words.
  • Read one tiny story.
  • Celebrate and stop.

A calm ten minutes is usually more useful than a long, tired session. Read more about reading practice.

Help without taking over

When your child pauses, ask for the first sound or a known part. These calm reading prompts can help, especially when your child starts guessing words.

The big idea: Home phonics should feel clear, short, and repeatable: one sound, a few words, one tiny story.
